PROJECT OVERVIEW
	A web-based hospital management system developed during my internship at Oda Bultum University.
	The system centralizes patient registration, clinical workflows, laboratory and radiology services, pharmacy, inventory, payments, reports, and user access control.
	The main goal is to reduce manual work, improve information flow, and support accurate hospital operations.

PROBLEM STATEMENT
	Hospital information is often distributed across paper files and disconnected processes.
	Patients may be registered repeatedly or lose time moving between departments.
	Clinical departments may not receive requests and payment status at the right time.
	Manual billing and inventory records increase errors and reduce accountability.
	Management needs timely reports for operational decisions.

PROJECT OBJECTIVES
	Develop a centralized system for hospital information management.
	Improve patient registration, triage, OPD, and IPD workflows.
	Connect laboratory and radiology requests to cashier payment verification.
	Support pharmacy sales, prescriptions, inventory, and stock transactions.
	Provide role-based access for administrators and hospital staff.
	Generate receipts and operational reports.
	Preserve medical history and protect important patient records.

SCOPE OF THE SYSTEM
	Patient management: registration, profile, status, contact and medical details.
	Patient flow: triage, OPD encounters, IPD admission, transfer and discharge.
	Clinical services: laboratory requests, results, radiology exams and reports.
	Finance: service requests, cashier queue, payments, insurance share and receipts.
	Pharmacy: prescriptions, sales, stock, inventory transactions and reporting.
	Administration: users, staff, doctors, nurses, departments, services and settings.
	Patient portal: patient access to personal information and selected services.

SYSTEM WORKFLOW
	1. Patient is registered and sent to triage.
	2. Triage sends the patient to OPD or another care pathway.
	3. OPD may send the patient to IPD when admission is required.
	4. Doctor or department requests laboratory or radiology service.
	5. Laboratory or radiology staff review the request and send it to cashier.
	6. Cashier collects or verifies payment and the department is notified.
	7. Specialist performs the service and records the result.
	8. Doctor and authorized staff access the completed clinical information.

KEY DESIGN DECISION: DEPARTMENT-TO-CASHIER FLOW
	OPD and IPD create the clinical queue item only.
	Laboratorist or radiologist selects the correct billable service.
	Only the specialist queue creates the service request for cashier.
	Cashier receives a clear patient share and payment request.
	After payment is cleared, the department can process the test or examination.
	This prevents duplicate payment requests and keeps clinical and financial responsibility clear.

TECHNOLOGIES USED
	Backend: PHP 8.2 and Laravel 12.
	Admin interface: Filament 5.
	Database: MySQL with migrations and foreign-key relationships.
	Frontend assets: Blade templates, Vite, Tailwind CSS and JavaScript.
	Authentication and authorization: Laravel authentication and Filament Shield / Spatie permissions.
	Reporting: Laravel views, CSV export and PDF receipt/report generation.
	Development tools: Composer, NPM, Artisan, Pest and Laravel Pint.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
	Presentation layer: Filament resources, tables, forms, actions and dashboards.
	Application layer: Laravel pages, controllers, services, events and notifications.
	Domain layer: Patient, OPD, IPD, Laboratory, Radiology, Pharmacy, Payment and Inventory models.
	Data layer: MySQL tables, migrations, relationships, indexes and foreign-key rules.
	The architecture separates interface, workflow logic and persistent hospital data.

SECURITY AND DATA INTEGRITY
	Role-based permissions limit users to their responsibilities.
	Patients cannot be hard-deleted when medical records reference them.
	Important relationships use foreign keys to protect data consistency.
	Payment and clinical status control whether a service can proceed.
	Sensitive pages and receipt routes require authentication.
	Validation is applied to forms before data is stored.

RESULTS AND BENEFITS
	A centralized workflow reduces dependence on paper records.
	Departments can see queues and patient information more consistently.
	Cashier receives structured requests with patient and insurance amounts.
	Payment verification controls laboratory and radiology processing.
	Receipts and reports improve financial traceability.
	Inventory and pharmacy records support stock accountability.
	The system provides a foundation for future hospital digital transformation.

CHALLENGES AND SOLUTIONS
	Challenge: different departments need different workflows.
	Solution: Filament resources and role-specific actions.
	Challenge: payment requests could be duplicated.
	Solution: create cashier requests only from laboratory and radiology queues.
	Challenge: deleting patients can break medical history.
	Solution: preserve records and use inactive or deceased status instead.
	Challenge: status values must match database rules.
	Solution: align workflow states with migration enum definitions.
	Challenge: many modules share patient data.
	Solution: Eloquent relationships, migrations and indexed foreign keys.
